## Notes

- "A pond full of Daphnia is clearer" holds when there are enough Daphnia; fish that eat Daphnia, or algae too big or toxic for them to eat (some cyanobacteria), can stop the effect. Keep the wording "a pond full of Daphnia", not "any pond with Daphnia".
- "All day long": filtering is continuous, but the rate depends on temperature and food; fine as a simplification.
- Replace the scite.ai link for Lampert et al. (1986) with the journal DOI page when checking.
- "Before they are allowed near rivers and lakes" simplifies regulatory testing: Daphnia tests are one standard part of chemical safety assessment in many countries, not a universal gate. Keep the wording general.
- Footage: the line-art Daphnia reel (filter-feeding loop) could be reused as the visual.
